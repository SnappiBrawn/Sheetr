1. Cells & Batteries

🔋 The Electric Cell

A portable source of electrical energy. It converts stored chemical energy into electrical energy.

🔌 What is a Battery?

A combination of two or more cells connected in a specific sequence.

⚠️ Rule: The positive (+) terminal of one cell must always connect to the negative (-) terminal of the next cell!

💡 Fact: Mobile phones use compact custom rechargeable batteries.

Light Emitters

2. Lamps vs LEDs

💡 Incandescent Bulb

Contains a very thin wire called a filament which gets extremely hot and glows when current passes through.

🚫 Fused Bulb: Occurs when the filament breaks, stopping the flow of current.

✨ LED (Light Emitting Diode)

Modern torches use LEDs instead of traditional bulbs because they are more efficient & durable.

Longer wire:
Positive (+) Terminal
Shorter wire:
Negative (-) Terminal

Current can only flow through an LED in ONE direction!

💡 Pro-Tip: LEDs don't have filaments, so they don't burn out easily!

The Circuit

4. Flow of Current

An electrical circuit provides a complete, continuous loop for electric current to flow through.

Closed Circuit (ON)

The path is complete/closed. Current flows from the positive (+) terminal of the cell, through the device, to the negative (-) terminal. The lamp glows.

Open Circuit (OFF)

There is a gap in the circuit. The path is broken, stopping the flow of electric current. The lamp does not glow.

💡 Direction Rule: Electric current historically flows from the POSITIVE (+) terminal → NEGATIVE (-) terminal!
🔧 Switch: A simple helper device that completes or breaks the path at your command.

Dive Deeper

6. Power Types

How does power from a portable cell differ from the electricity coming from your household wall sockets?

Direct Current (DC) Low Risk

Flows in one single direction. Usually supplied by chemical batteries or solar cells. Typically powers smaller, portable gadgets.

Alternating Current (AC) High Danger

Changes direction back and forth periodically. Generated by large power plants & delivered via wall sockets. Powers heavy home appliances.

⚠️ Caution: Mains electricity (AC) is highly dangerous! Never experiment with household wall plug sockets.
