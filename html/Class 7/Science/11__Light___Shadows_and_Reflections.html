⚡ LIGHT: SHADOWS & REFLECTIONS Quick Reference Summary

All the essential concepts, visual diagrams, and fun facts curated in an ultimate high-density student revision canvas!

1. Sources of Light

Light is a form of energy that makes things visible to us. Things that produce light are divided into two clear categories:

Luminous Objects

Objects that emit/give out light of their own.

Sun (Natural) Firefly (Natural) LED Lamp (Artificial)

Non-Luminous Objects

Objects that do NOT emit light themselves; we only see them when they reflect light from a luminous source.

Moon Textbook Chair

Warning: Moon is non-luminous! It only reflects sunlight.

2. Rectilinear Propagation

"Light travels in a Straight Line!"

Proof Activities:

  • A.
    The Alignment Test: Aligning three matchboxes with holes in the exact same spot allows you to see torchlight. Shift even one box slightly sideways, and light is instantly blocked!
  • B.
    The Pipe Trick:
    Straight Pipe → Can see candle flame
    Bent Pipe → Cannot see candle flame!
Thus, light doesn't bend around obstacles in our everyday environment.

3. Materials & Light

How materials interact with light determines if we can see through them:

Transparent

Passes light fully. We can see through them clearly.

Glass, Clear Water, Air
Translucent

Passes light partially. See-through is blurry/hazy.

Tracing Paper, Butter Paper, Frosted Glass
Opaque

Blocks light completely. Cannot see through at all!

Cardboard, Metal, Thick Cloth, Wood

4. Shadow Dynamics

Definition: A dark patch formed on a screen when an opaque object blocks light traveling in a straight line.

3 Essential Requirements:

Light Source
Opaque Object
Screen (Wall/Floor)

Key Shadow Properties:

  • Always Dark: No matter the color of the object, the shadow is always black!
  • Variable Size: Moves closer to the light source → Larger. Moves closer to screen → Smaller.
  • Misleading Shapes: A shadow can tell us shape details but can also trick us (e.g., hand shadow puppetry).

5. Reflection & Mirrors

What is Reflection? The change in direction of a light beam when it falls on a shiny, highly polished surface like a mirror.

Properties of Images in a Plane Mirror:

  • Same Size: Image is exactly as big as the object.
  • Erect: Upright (head stays top, feet bottom).
  • Lateral Inversion: Left side of object becomes right side of image & vice-versa.
  • Equal Distance: The image is as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it.
  • Virtual: Cannot be caught on a screen.

6. Pinhole Camera

A simple optical device without any lens that forms real, interesting images on a tracing paper screen.

Mechanism of Construction:

Two cardboard boxes (one smaller, sliding inside the larger). One end of the large box has a tiny pinhole. The corresponding end of the small box is cut out and covered with a translucent tracing paper screen.

Image Characteristics:

Inverted (Upside down) Colored (Not black) Real Image Shows sun's shape

7. Optical Devices

Cool devices we can build using multiple flat mirrors and light reflection!

Periscope

A Z-shaped tube containing two plane mirrors mounted parallel facing each other at a 45° angle.

Used in submarines, army bunkers, or to see over crowds.

Kaleidoscope

Contains three rectangular plane mirror strips joined in a triangle inside a cylinder with colored beads/glass bits at one end.

Multiple reflections generate beautiful, non-repeating symmetrical patterns.
