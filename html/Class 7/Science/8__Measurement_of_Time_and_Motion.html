Chapter 8: Measurement of Time & Motion

History

Ancient Clocks

Before modern wristwatches, humans tracked repetitive natural cycles (Sunrise, Moon phases, changing Seasons).

  • Sundials: Tracks local "solar time" using the changing position of a shadow cast by the Sun.
    Samrat Yantra (Jaipur) is the world's largest stone sundial (27m tall, accurate to 2s!).
  • Water Clocks (Ghatika-yantra): Uses regular flow of water in/out of a marked vessel. Highly used in ancient India.
  • Hourglass: Measures fixed durations via sand flowing between two glass bulbs.
  • Candle Clocks: Marked candles that indicate time intervals as they burn down.
Core Concept

The Simple Pendulum

Consists of a small metallic ball (Bob) suspended from a rigid support by a light, inextensible string.

Oscillation path
Extreme (B) ← Mean (O) → Extreme (A)
One full oscillation: O → A → B → O
  • Periodic Motion: Repeats itself at equal intervals.
  • Time Period: Time taken to complete exactly one oscillation.
Super Important!
The time period depends only on the length of the pendulum. It does not change with the mass of the bob!
Evolution

Clocks & Precision

Galileo's Insight

Noticed a swinging cathedral lamp matched his constant pulse. Concluded pendulum period is constant for a given length.

Huygens' Breakthrough (1656)

First to design and patent the highly accurate pendulum-driven clock.

Modern Timekeeping

Quartz Clocks: Driven by extremely rapid vibrations of a quartz crystal.

Atomic Clocks: Absurdly precise, losing only 1 second in millions of years!

Writing Guidelines:
  • Always write unit symbols in lowercase (except derived from names, e.g., 's', 'min', 'h').
  • Never use plurals for symbols (e.g., 10 s, NOT 10 secs; 5 h, NOT 5 hrs).
  • Never add a full stop after a unit symbol unless it's the end of a sentence.
Formula Board

What is Speed?

The distance covered by an object in a unit of time. Generally indicates how slow or fast an object is moving.

Speed =
Total Distance Covered Total Time Taken
SI Unit: meters per second m/s
Other Unit: kilometers per hour km/h
Fast Conversion Hack:
km/h → m/s
× 5 / 18
m/s → km/h
× 18 / 5
Math Corner

Calculations Helper

Find Distance
D = Speed × Time
Find Time
T = Distance / Speed
Quick Example Solved:

Q: Swati rides 3.6 km on her bicycle in 15 minutes. Find her speed in m/s.

Distance = 3.6 km = 3.6 × 1000 = 3600 m
Time = 15 min = 15 × 60 = 900 s
Speed = 3600 m / 900 s = 4 m/s
Dashboard

Measuring Devices

Speedometer

Measures the speed of a vehicle instantaneously in km/h directly at any given moment.

Odometer

Measures the total distance traveled by the vehicle in kilometers (km).

Motion Types

Linear Motion

Motion of an object moving along a straight line.

Uniform Linear Motion

An object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time. Speed remains perfectly constant.

(An idealization - rare in everyday life!)

Non-Uniform Linear Motion

An object covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time. Speed is constantly changing.

(E.g., driving a car through heavy city traffic)
At a Glance

In a Nutshell

  • The time taken by a pendulum to complete one full oscillation is called its time period.
  • The basic SI unit of time is the second (s).
  • The basic SI unit of speed is meters per second (m/s).
  • Average speed = Total distance covered divided by total time taken.
  • Speedometer displays speed in km/h, and Odometer logs total distance in km.
