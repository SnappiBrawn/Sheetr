Essential Life Processes

⭐

Processes essential for the survival of living organisms collectively called life processes:

  • Nutrition (Obtaining energy)
  • Respiration (Releasing energy)
  • Excretion (Waste removal)
  • Reproduction (Continuity of life)
This chapter focuses heavily on Nutrition and Respiration.

The Digestive Journey

Human Alimentary Canal

Food travels through a continuous long tube called the alimentary canal:

1 Mouth Cavity (Mechanical breakdown + Saliva)
↓
2 Oesophagus (Food Pipe) (Peristalsis)
↓
3 Stomach (Chyme creation + Protein digestion)
↓
4 Small Intestine (Absorption & Final Digestion)
↓
5 Large Intestine (Water & salt absorption)
↓
6 Rectum & Anus (Storage & Egestion of stool)

Mouth & Saliva

Experiment
Mechanical Digestion Teeth crush and chew food into fine pieces.
Saliva & Starch Saliva contains digestive juices that break down starch (complex carb) into sugar (simpler forms).
Iodine Test on Rice
Test Tube A Boiled Rice + Iodine → Blue-Black (Starch is Present)
Test Tube B Chewed Rice + Iodine → No Color Change (Saliva broke down Starch)

The Stomach Chamber

Chemical Factory
Peristalsis & Churning Walls gently contract and relax in wave-like motions to push food down and mix it with secretions.

The inner lining of stomach secretes three crucial fluids:

  • Digestive Juice: Breaks down complex proteins into simpler components.
  • Hydrochloric Acid (HCl): Kills harmful bacteria entering with food and creates an acidic medium for digestion.
  • Mucus: Protects the delicate inner lining of the stomach from its own strong acid.

Small Intestine (Villi)

~6 Meters Long!

The longest part of the digestive tract, receiving secretions from 3 sources:

1. Liver (Biles) Secretes basic Bile. Neutralizes acid and emulsifies fats into tiny droplets.
2. Pancreas Secretes pancreatic juice. Breaks down carbs, proteins, and fats.
Villi: Absorption Experts

Thousands of tiny finger-like projections on the inner lining. Villi vastly increase the surface area for efficient absorption of digested nutrients into blood vessels.

Animal Adaptations

Evolution
Ruminants (Cows, Buffaloes)

Partially chew grass and swallow to a chamber called the Rumen. It is later brought back to the mouth as cud for slow chewing (Rumination).

Birds (Gizzard)

Birds have no teeth! They rely on a strong muscular organ called the Gizzard containing ingested grit (small stones) to crush food.

Breathing vs Respiration

Key Concept
Breathing Physical Process

Inhaling oxygen-rich air & exhaling carbon dioxide.

Respiration Chemical Process

Breaking down food inside cells to release energy.

Chemical Formula Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon Dioxide + Water + Energy

How We Breathe

Mechanical Model
Inhalation (Breathing In)
  • Ribs move up & outwards.
  • Diaphragm moves downwards (flattens).
  • Chest cavity expands → Air rushed into lungs.
Exhalation (Breathing Out)
  • Ribs move down & inwards.
  • Diaphragm moves upwards (returns to dome shape).
  • Chest cavity contracts → Air pushed out.

Gas Exchange

Alveoli

Exchange occurs in balloon-like sacs called alveoli, rich with blood vessels.

Gas Inhaled Air Exhaled Air
Oxygen (O2) ~21% ~16-17%
Carbon Dioxide (CO2) ~0.04% ~4-5%
The Lime Water Test Exhaling into lime water turns it milky/cloudy, demonstrating carbon dioxide release!

Respiration Adaptations

Diversity
🐟
Fish: Gills

Gills absorb dissolved oxygen directly from water passing through them.

🐸
Frogs (Amphibians)

Tadpoles use gills. Adult frogs use lungs on land and moist skin in water.

🪱
Earthworms

Breathe completely through their thin, moist skin for direct gas exchange.

Science, Society & Factoids

Fascinating Tidbits
Alexis & Beaumont (1822)

Dr. William Beaumont discovered digestion by performing experiments through a shotgun wound in patient Alexis St. Martin's stomach that never fully closed!

Celiac Disease

An autoimmune condition where eating gluten (wheat protein) damages the small intestine's villi, making it hard to absorb vital nutrients.

Pranayama & Yoga

Ancient breathing techniques such as Pranayama and Tummo breathing are practiced globally to improve lung health, balance, and focus.
