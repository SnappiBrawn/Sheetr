MEASUREMENT & MOTION

Physics Study Guide • Chapter 5 Quick Review

1. Measurement Basics

In ancient times, people used inconsistent body parts to measure:

  • Angula: Finger width width.
  • Handspan (Balisht): Stretch of palm.
  • Strides & Foot: Walking pacing steps.

The Problem:

Body dimensions vary per person! This created massive confusion, necessitating a universal standard.

2. SI Units & Conversion

The worldwide standard system is the International System of Units (SI Units).

SI Unit of Length = Metre (m)

Kilometer 1 km = 1000 m
Centimeter 1 m = 100 cm
Millimeter 1 cm = 10 mm
Imperial Inch 1 inch = 2.54 cm

3. Measurement Guide

Placement:

Place the scale parallel and in complete contact with the object along its entire length.

Eye Alignment:

Your eye position must be directly vertically above the target point. Looking from angles causes parallax error!

Eye (A) ← WRONG | Eye (B) ↓ CORRECT | Eye (C) → WRONG

Broken Scales:

If the zero end is worn/broken, measure from another mark (e.g., 1.0 cm):

True Length = End Value − Start Value

Example: 10.4 cm − 1.0 cm = 9.4 cm

4. Curves & Positions

Measuring Curved Lines:

You cannot use a straight wooden ruler directly!

The Thread Trick:

  1. Trace along the curve carefully using a flexible thread.
  2. Mark the start and endpoints on the thread.
  3. Stretch the thread straight along a standard scale to measure the length.

What is a Reference Point?

A fixed, stationary location used to state the position or observe the motion of an object.

Example: A bus stand, milestone, or gate post from which distances are measured.

5. Rest & Motion

Motion:

An object is said to be in motion if its position changes with respect to a fixed reference point over time.

Rest:

An object is said to be at rest if its position remains unchanged relative to a fixed reference point over time.

Relative Nature of Motion:

A passenger inside a moving bus is at rest relative to other passengers inside, but in motion relative to trees outside!

6. Types of Motion

Linear Motion

Movement along a perfectly straight line path.

e.g., Falling apple, marching troops, sliding box

Circular Motion

Movement of an object along a circular trajectory path.

e.g., Hands of a clock, merry-go-round, spinning top

Oscillatory Motion

Continuous to-and-fro movement about a fixed, stable position.

e.g., Simple pendulum, swing, vibrating ruler

Periodic Motion

Any motion that repeats its path after exact, fixed intervals of time.

Note: Circular & Oscillatory motions are often periodic.
