9. Methods of Separation

...in Everyday Life 🌾🏺🔬

"साधु ऐसा चाहिए जैसा सूप सुभाय ।
सार सार को गहि रहे थोथा दई उड़ाय ।।"

"Just like winnowing makes the husk blow away while retaining the grains, sages let the virtues stay while discarding the vices." — Kabir

Why Separate?

We separate components of a mixture (combination of 2+ substances) for two major reasons:

To Remove

Non-useful or harmful components (e.g. stones from rice)

To Separate

Two different but highly useful components (e.g. butter from milk)

💡 Key Concept: Separation is based on physical property differences (size, color, weight, magnetism, state)!
Dry Methods (Part 1) Solid-Solid

1. Handpicking

Picking out impurities by hand from a mixture.

  • Criteria: Impurities must look different in size, shape, or color.
  • Condition: Impurities must be in small quantities.
  • Example: Separating stones/husk from rice, wheat, or pulses.

2. Threshing

Separating grains from harvested dry stalks.

Action Beating stalks against a hard object (wooden log) or using bullocks/machines called Threshers.

🌾 Helps extract thousands of tiny grains quickly!

Dry Methods (Part 2) Solid-Solid

3. Winnowing

Separating lighter components from heavier ones using wind/blowing air.

🌾 Heavier Grains → Fall straight down in a heap.

💨 Lighter Husk → Blown away by the wind, forming a separate pile.

🛠️ Traditional Tool: Bamboo tray called a soop.

4. Sieving

Using a sieve to separate solid particles of different sizes.

  • Fine particles pass through the holes; larger impurities remain on top.
  • Examples: Removing bran from flour; separating pebbles and stones from sand at construction sites.
Liquid Separations Insoluble Solids

Sedimentation

The process where the heavier, insoluble solid settles to the bottom of the container when left undisturbed.

Decantation

Pouring out the upper clean liquid into another vessel without disturbing the settled sediment.

💡 Also used to separate immiscible liquids (e.g., Oil & Water layer).

Churning (Centrifugation)

Vigorously spinning or shaking liquid mixtures like curd or milk using a wooden churner (mathni).

Result: Lighter butter floats to the top, buttermilk stays below!

Filtration Fine Particles

Separating insoluble solids from a liquid using a medium with tiny pores that allow only the liquid to pass.

☕ Residue: The solid trapped on the filter (e.g. tea leaves, mud).
🥛 Filtrate: The clear liquid passing through (e.g. clear tea, filtered water).

Available Filter Mediums:

Strainer

Fine Cloth

Filter Paper

Evaporation Soluble Solids

Converting a liquid into gas (vapor) to separate a dissolved solid from a liquid.

How We Obtain Salt

🌊 Seawater is collected in shallow pits.
☀️ Sunlight & Wind evaporate the water.
🧂 Solid salt remains behind for purification!

💡 Did you know? The water bodies like Sambhar Lake in Rajasthan are huge natural sources of common salt in India!

Magnetic Separation

Separating magnetic materials (like iron) from non-magnetic mixtures (like sawdust, sand, or municipal waste).

In Modern Recycling:

Gigantic magnets fitted to cranes are lowered over heaps of scrap waste to attract and separate scrap iron from plastic, wood, and concrete.

⭐ Tip: If a carpenter drops iron nails into sawdust, a simple bar magnet will pull out all the nails instantly without sorting through sawdust manually!
Fun Facts & DIY

🫖 Evolution of Tea Bags

First made of silk (which held leaves well but did not fall apart in hot water), then gauze/muslin, and finally modern filter paper.

🌀 Custom Water Filters

Apart from filter paper, materials like cotton, charcoal, and layers of sand & gravel are highly effective natural filters used since ancient times.

🚫 Avoid plastic pollution in our water bodies! Keep rivers and oceans clean for marine life.
