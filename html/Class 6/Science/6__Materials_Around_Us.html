Matter vs. Material

Matter:

Anything that occupies space and has mass.
(e.g., Water, sand, pebbles, air, cup)

Material:

Any substance used to create or make objects.

One Object → Multiple Materials:

A Tumbler can be made of: Glass, Plastic, Metal, or Wood!

Classification

The method of arranging objects into groups based on a common property they share.

Sorting Parameters:

  • Shape
  • Colour
  • Hardness
  • Lustre (Shine)

*Grouping helps us study and observe patterns in the properties of things.

Appearance & Hardness

1. Lustre (Shiny vs Dull)

  • Lustrous (Shiny): Metals like Iron, Copper, Aluminium, Gold.
  • Non-Lustrous: Paper, Wood, Rubber, Chalk.
Metals can lose their shine over time due to the action of air & moisture! Look at freshly cut surfaces!

2. Hardness (Hard vs Soft)

  • Hard: Difficult to compress or scratch (e.g., Stone, Metal Key, Brick).
  • Soft: Easily compressed or scratched (e.g., Sponge, Eraser).

Transparency

CLEAR

Transparent

Things seen clearly. (e.g., Glass, Water, Air, Cellophane)

HAZY

Translucent

Things seen, but not clearly. (e.g., Butter paper, Frosted glass)

BLOCK

Opaque

Cannot see through at all. (e.g., Wood, Cardboard, Metals)

Is water transparent?

Yes! But it can be made opaque (e.g. by mixing mud/paint).

Solubility in Water

Soluble

Disappears completely in water.

Sugar, Salt, Oxygen Gas.

Insoluble

Does not disappear even after stirring.

Sand, Sawdust, Chalk, Oil.

Make Your Own ORS!

Oral Rehydration Solution (helps treat severe dehydration due to diarrhoea):

1 Litre Boiled Water + 6 tsp Sugar + 0.5 tsp Common Salt

Mass & Volume Basics

Mass SI Unit: kg

Quantity of matter present in an object.

  • Measured in: gram (g), kilogram (kg).
  • Space format: Leave a space between number and unit (e.g., 7 kg, not 7kg).

Volume SI Unit: m³

The space occupied by matter.

  • Measured in: Litre (L), Millilitre (mL), Cubic Metre (m³).
  • Conversion: 1 m³ = 1000 L

Ancient Wisdom & Pottery History

Earliest Pottery (7,000 to 8,000 years old)

Found in the Ganga plains (Lahuradewa) and Baluchistan (Mehrgarh). Baked clay pottery is called terracotta. Used for cooking and storage of food grains, oils, ghee.

Ayurvedic 10 Pairs of Opposites (Guñas)

From Ashtanga Hridaya Sutrasthana - used to describe physical matter:

(i) guru (heavy) × laghu (light)
(ii) manda (slow) × tikshna (quick)
(iii) hima (cold) × ushna (hot)
(iv) snigdha (unctous) × ruksha (dry)
(v) shlakshana (smooth) × khara (rough)
(vi) sandra (solid) × drava (liquid)
(vii) mridu (soft) × kathina (hard)
(viii) sthira (stable) × khala (mobile)
