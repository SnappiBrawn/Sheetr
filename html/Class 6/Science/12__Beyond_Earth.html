Stars & Constellations

Stars shine with their own light. Groups of stars forming recognizable patterns are called Constellations.

More to Know: Boundaries were defined in the early 20th century by the International Astronomical Union (IAU). There are exactly 88 official constellations.
  • Orion (The Hunter): Easily spotted in winter. Features three bright middle stars forming the "belt". Contains Betelgeuse (known in Indian Astronomy as Ardra).
  • Canis Major (Great Dog): Contains Sirius, the brightest star in the entire night sky. Locate it by drawing a line eastward through Orion’s belt!
  • Taurus (The Bull): Houses Aldebaran (known as Rohini) and the star cluster Pleiades (Krittika).

Celestial Roadmaps

Big Dipper vs. Little Dipper

Big Dipper: Part of Ursa Major (known as Saptarishi in India). Looks like a long-handled ladle or pot.
Little Dipper: Part of Ursa Minor. Contains the famous Pole Star.

Pole Star (Polaris / Dhruva tara)

Appears completely stationary in the North sky. Used heavily by ancient explorers for night navigation.

👉 How to locate: Look at the two pointer stars at the end of the Big Dipper's cup. Draw an imaginary straight line through them, extend it about 5x the distance, and you'll hit Polaris!
Tribal Lore: Central Indian tribes view the Big Dipper rectangle as a "grandmother's cot" with 3 thieves stealing it! Konkan fishermen see it as a "boat".

The Sun & Near Space

The Sun is a medium-sized star (known as Surya) that produces all of our system's heat and light. It is about 100 times larger than Earth in diameter.

Distance to Earth ~150 Million km (= 1 Astronomical Unit / au)
Next Nearest Star Proxima Centauri ~269,000 au away
Revolution: Movement of a body around the Sun. Earth takes ~1 year.
Rotation: Spinning on its own axis. Earth takes 24 hours (1 day).

⚠️ Pluto was reclassified as a Dwarf Planet by the IAU in 2006.

The Moon & Satellites

Any object revolving around a larger object is a satellite. The Moon is Earth's natural satellite.

🌕 Distance from Earth: ~3,84,000 km
🔄 Revolution Period: Takes ~27 days to orbit the Earth
🌪️ Atmosphere: Absolutely none! Features like craters remain frozen in time.
🕳️ Craters: Bowl-like structures caused by meteor/asteroid impacts.
Chandrayaan Missions (India)
  • Chandrayaan-1 (2008): India's first Moon orbiter.
  • Chandrayaan-2 (2019): High resolution mapping.
  • Chandrayaan-3 (July 2023): Landed the Vikram lander & Pragyan rover successfully near the Moon's South Pole on 23 August 2023!
🇮🇳 23rd August is celebrated as "National Space Day" in India!

Celestial Travelers

Asteroids (Minor Rocky Bodies)

Rocky, irregular bodies measuring from 10 m to 500 km. They orbit primarily in the Asteroid Belt situated between Mars and Jupiter.

Comets (Icy-Rocky Travelers)

Composed of ice, dust, and gases. As they approach the Sun, the frozen material evaporates, creating a brilliant, glowing tail pointing away from the Sun.

💫 Halley's Comet: Appears once every 76 years. Last seen in 1986.
🗣️ Local Names: Known as Dhumaketu, Pucchya-Taro (star with a tail), or Zendya-Taro.

The Great Beyond

The Milky Way Galaxy Known in India as Akasha Ganga. It is a massive spiral band containing billions of stars. Our solar system resides in one of its arms.
The Universe The sum total of all space, matter, energy, and billions of independent galaxies.
🛸 Exoplanets & Life: Scientists are searching for planets orbiting stars outside our solar system (exoplanets) to seek signs of extraterrestrial life. No conclusive evidence found yet!

Night Sky Watching

Light pollution, smoke, and tall buildings limit our stargazing. Find dark, cloudless, moonless open areas for the best view!

Stay under adult guidance in safe open spots.
Allow 30 minutes for your eyes to adjust to darkness.
Use sky-mapping apps like Stellarium or Sky Map.
Use binoculars or a telescope for dim cosmic entities.
⚠️ CAUTION: Never go to secluded dark spots at night without an adult!
